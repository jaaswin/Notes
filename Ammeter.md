# Ammeter - Detailed Explanation

## 1. Introduction

An **ammeter** is an electrical measuring instrument used to measure the **electric current flowing through a circuit**.

The word **ammeter** comes from:

> **Ampere + Meter = Ammeter**

The SI unit of electric current is the **Ampere (A)**.

An ammeter tells us how much electric charge is flowing through a circuit per unit time.

---

# 2. What is Electric Current?

Before understanding an ammeter, we need to understand **electric current**.

Electric current is the **rate of flow of electric charge** through a conductor.

It is represented by the symbol:

**I**

The unit is:

**Ampere (A)**

The basic formula is:

\[
I = \frac{Q}{t}
\]

Where:

- **I** = Current in amperes (A)
- **Q** = Electric charge in coulombs (C)
- **t** = Time in seconds (s)

### Example

Suppose 10 coulombs of charge flow through a conductor in 2 seconds.

\[
I = \frac{Q}{t}
\]

\[
I = \frac{10}{2}
\]

\[
I = 5A
\]

Therefore, the current flowing through the conductor is **5 A**.

---

# 3. Definition of Ammeter

An **ammeter** is an electrical instrument used to measure the amount of electric current flowing through a circuit.

### Simple Definition

> An ammeter is a device used to measure electric current in a circuit.

### Exam Definition

> An ammeter is an electrical measuring instrument used to measure the current flowing through an electrical circuit. It is connected in series with the circuit and has very low internal resistance.

Why is an ammeter connected in series?

Suppose a circuit has a resistor carrying 1 A.

If we want to measure that 1 A, the current must pass through the ammeter.

Why does an ammeter have low resistance?

An ammeter has very low internal resistance.

An ideal ammeter has:

Resistance = 0 Ω

A practical ammeter has resistance that is very close to zero.

Why?

Because the ammeter should not significantly reduce the current flowing through the circuit.

If its resistance were high, it would change the circuit current and give an incorrect measurement.

⚠️ What happens if we connect an ammeter in parallel?

Never connect an ammeter directly across a battery or voltage source.

An ammeter has very low resistance. Connecting it in parallel can create a short circuit, causing a very large current to flow.

This may:

Blow the meter's fuse
Damage the meter
Damage the circuit
Cause sparks or overheating
Remember:

Ammeter → Series

Voltmeter → Parallel

2. Digital Ammeter

A digital ammeter displays the current as numbers.

Example:

┌─────────────────┐
│      1.25 A     │
└─────────────────┘

Digital multimeters commonly include current measurement.

3. Clamp Ammeter

A clamp meter measures current by clamping around a conductor. It can measure current without physically breaking the circuit in many applications.

Ammeter and Multimeter

So, a multimeter can be used as an ammeter when the current function and correct input terminal are selected.
